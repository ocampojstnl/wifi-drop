Troubleshooting

Help

Most problems come down to the network: here’s how to fix them.

A device can’t open Wi-Fi Drop
  • Same Wi-Fi? The phone, tablet or computer must be on your home Wi-Fi, not mobile data or a guest network. Then the address shown on any device already in works on it too.
  • Firewall. When Windows asked about Node.js, it needed Allow on Private networks. Check in Windows Security → Firewall → Allow an app through firewall.
  • Network type. In Windows Settings → Network, your connection should be set to Private, not Public.
  • Several networks? On the Share page, pick the right one under the QR code.

“Couldn’t connect directly”

The two devices see each other on Wi-Fi Drop but can’t reach each other. Many routers have a setting that keeps Wi-Fi devices apart, called AP isolation, client isolation or wireless isolation: turn it off in the router’s settings. Guest networks always keep devices apart.

A transfer stopped

Wi-Fi Drop picks up where it stopped when the connection comes back: keep both pages open. Phones pause the browser when the screen turns off, so keep the screen on during big transfers.

{{ad3}}
Making transfers faster
  • Use 5 GHz Wi-Fi. If your router has a network ending in “5G”, connect the phone to it: often 3–5× faster than 2.4 GHz.
  • Get closer to the router. Walls and distance cut Wi-Fi speed quickly.
  • Check the computer’s cable. A wired PC stuck at 100 Mbps (Settings → Network → Ethernet) caps transfers at about 11 MB/s: a Cat5e or Cat6 cable usually brings it to 1 Gbps.
  • Keep the screen on on phones during a transfer.

A received file on a phone

Phones keep a received file until you tap Save; it then goes to your Downloads. Very large files (over about 1.5 GB) may not fit in a phone’s memory: send those to a computer instead.
